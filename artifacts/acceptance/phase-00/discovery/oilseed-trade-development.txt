                                                 India’sShifting                                                                                  Vegetable Oil Suppliers

           In marketing year 2025/26 (Oct-Sep), the world’s largest vegetable oil importer no longer has the
           world’s largest vegetable oil exporter as its main trading partner. For decades, Indonesia
           commanded the largest share of                the Indian import market  for vegetable oil. However, Argentina
           and Malaysia have surged past and displaced Indonesia to third place, with other countries
           including Brazil and Thailand also gaining market share  in India.

           Palm oil is India’s most imported oil, and until recently, Indonesia has been the historic preeminent
           vegetable oil supplier to India due to its dominance in the   global palm oil trade. Indonesia’s t rade
           with India has been declining since 2022/23 due to the            rapid implementation of its                          increasingly
           large biodiesel blending mandates, which has  resulted in a larger palm oil domestic                        consumption.
           In 2025/26, Malaysia and Thailand have increased shipments of palm oil to India, both aided by
           stronger production than the year prior                      and price competitiveness  against Indonesian origin.

EXPORT PRICES
Soybean export prices steadily declined over the last month as the U.S. harvest and Brazil planting
progressed without major issues. Brazil soybeans maintained a seasonal price premium over U.S. -
origin, though  the gap narrowed somewhat            on a depreciating real following Brazil ’s runoff elections
in late October. Soybean meal export prices fell sharply for major exporters, with U.S. soybean
meal dropping from a peak of $450/ton in mid-October to below $400/ton during the first week of
November. This decline followed harvest pressure  on soybeans and acceleration of U.S. crushing.

U.S. soybean oil export prices declined throughout                                the month on soybean harvest pressure,
accelerated crushing, and falling petroleum prices in early November                      which put downward
pressure on prices of soybean and palm oil for all exporters. Brazil and Argentina soybean oil
continued to trade at a discount to U.S. oil             and were largely rangebound before dipping in late
October following the depreciation of                  the real. Indonesia and Malaysia palm oil export prices both
remained below soybean oil over the month and dropped sharply in early November on reports of
large October palm oil stocks in Malaysia.

OTHER NOTES

Marketing Years (MY):  MY refers to the 12-month period at the onset of the main harvest, when the
crop is marketed (i.e., consumed, traded, or stored).   The year first listed begins a country's MY for
that commodity (2022/23 starts in 2022); except for oilseeds           in certain Southern Hemisphere
countries, where the second year begins the MY (2022/23 starts in 2023). For a complete list of
local marketing years, please see the FAS website (https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/): go to
Reports, Reference Data, and then Data Availability.

Stocks: Unless otherwise stated, stock data are based on an aggregate of differing local marketing
years and should not be construed as representing world stock levels at a fixed point in time.

Consumption: World totals for consumption reflect total utilization, including food, seed,
industrial, feed, and waste; as well as differences in local marketing year imports and local
marketing year exports.  Consumption statistics for regions and individual countries, however,
reflect food, seed, industrial, feed, and waste only.

Trade: All PSD tables are balanced on the different local marketing years.
